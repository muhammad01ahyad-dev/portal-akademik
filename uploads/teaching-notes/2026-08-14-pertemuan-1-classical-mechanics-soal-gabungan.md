## Answer Key & Explanations

**Q1 — ③** T(K) = 27 + 273 = 300 K.

**Q2 — ④** Q = mcΔT = 500 × 4.2 × 60 = 126,000 J = 126 kJ.

**Q3 — ①** Heat lost by metal = heat gained by water. 200 × c_m × (100−25) = 300 × 4.2 × (25−20) → 200 × c_m × 75 = 6300 → c_m = 6300/15000 = 0.42 J/(g·K).

**Q4 — ②** During a phase change (melting), all added heat goes into latent heat, so temperature stays constant until melting is complete.

**Q5 — ②** ΔU = Q − W = 500 − 200 = 300 J.

**Q6 — ②** Adiabatic means Q = 0, no heat exchange with surroundings.

**Q7 — ②** Work done = 800 − 500 = 300 J. Efficiency = W/Q_h = 300/800 = 0.375 = 37.5%.

**Q9 — ③** Boyle's law: PV = constant. V → V/3 ⇒ P → 3P.

**Q10 — ③** Charles's law: V/T = constant at fixed P. T doubles ⇒ V doubles.

**Q11 — ③** P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ → (1×10⁵)(2.0)/300 = (2×10⁵)(V₂)/600 → V₂ = [(1×10⁵)(2.0)(600)] / [300 × 2×10⁵] = 4.0 L.

**Q12 — ①** Ideal gas law for 1 mole: PV = RT.

**Q13 — ②** Gas pressure arises from molecular collisions with the container walls (kinetic theory).

**Q14 — ③** For a monatomic ideal gas, U = (3/2)nRT.

**Q15 — ②** At constant volume, W = 0, so by the first law, Q = ΔU: all heat added increases internal energy.

**Q16 — ③** Isothermal ⇒ ΔT = 0 ⇒ ΔU = 0 (ideal gas), so Q = W by the first law.

**Q17 — ⑤** Q = ΔU + W = 450 + 300 = 750 J.

**Q18 — ③** Diatomic molecules have rotational (and sometimes vibrational) degrees of freedom in addition to translational ones, giving higher internal energy at the same T and n.

**Q19 — ②** Adiabatic (Q=0) expansion: gas does positive work using its own internal energy, so temperature drops.

**Q20 — ②** Carnot efficiency = 1 − T_c/T_h = 1 − 300/500 = 1 − 0.6 = 0.4 = 40%.

# PART I — MECHANICS

## 1. Motion and Force

### 1.1 Description of Motion
- **Position, displacement**: displacement is a vector (change in position); distance is scalar.
- **Velocity**: v = Δx/Δt (average); instantaneous velocity is the slope of x-t graph.
- **Acceleration**: a = Δv/Δt; slope of v-t graph.
- **Equations of uniformly accelerated motion:**
  - v = v₀ + at
  - x = v₀t + ½at²
  - v² = v₀² + 2ax
- **Relative motion**: v(A relative to B) = v_A − v_B (vector subtraction).
- **Free fall**: a = g (≈ 9.8 m/s²), downward, no initial horizontal velocity.
- **Projectile motion**: horizontal velocity constant; vertical motion is free fall. Treat x and y independently, same t.

### 1.2 Various Forces
- **Gravity (weight)**: W = mg.
- **Normal force**: perpendicular to contact surface.
- **Friction**:
  - Static: f_s ≤ μ_s N (prevents motion up to a maximum)
  - Kinetic: f_k = μ_k N (opposes relative sliding, μ_k < μ_s generally)
- **Tension**: pulling force along a string/cable, same magnitude throughout an ideal (massless, frictionless-pulley) string.
- **Elastic (spring) force**: F = −kx (Hooke's law), restoring, opposite to displacement.
- **Force from fluids**: buoyancy, pressure forces.

### 1.3 Equilibrium of Forces
- A point is in equilibrium when the **vector sum of all forces = 0**.
- **Resolution**: break a force into perpendicular components (Fx, Fy).
- **Resultant**: vector sum of multiple forces.
- Solve equilibrium problems by writing ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0 separately.

### 1.4 Equilibrium of Rigid Bodies
- **Torque (moment of force)**: τ = F × d (d = perpendicular distance from pivot to line of action).
- **Conditions for rigid-body equilibrium**: ΣF = 0 **and** Στ = 0 (about any point).
- **Couple**: pair of equal, opposite, parallel forces not sharing a line of action; produces pure rotation, net torque = F × (distance between lines).
- **Center of mass**: the point where the entire weight can be considered to act; for uniform objects, it's the geometric center.

### 1.5 Newton's Laws
- **1st law (inertia)**: an object stays at rest or constant velocity unless acted on by a net external force.
- **2nd law**: F = ma (net force = mass × acceleration); this is a vector equation.
- **3rd law**: for every action force there is an equal, opposite reaction force acting on the other body.
- **Units**: 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². Dimensional analysis: [F] = MLT⁻².

### 1.6 Motion with Friction / Air Resistance
- Kinetic friction opposes relative sliding motion; magnitude μ_k N, independent of speed (idealized).
- **Air resistance**: often modeled as proportional to velocity (or v²) at higher speeds.
- **Terminal velocity**: reached when air resistance balances gravity (net force = 0), so acceleration = 0 and speed becomes constant.

---

## 2. Energy and Momentum

### 2.1 Work and Kinetic Energy
- **Work**: W = F·d·cosθ (θ = angle between force and displacement).
- **Work–energy theorem**: net work done on an object = its change in kinetic energy: W_net = ΔKE.
- **Kinetic energy**: KE = ½mv².
- **Power**: P = W/t = F·v (for constant force along motion direction).

### 2.2 Potential Energy
- **Gravitational PE**: U = mgh (h measured from a reference level).
- **Elastic PE**: U = ½kx² (spring compressed/stretched by x).
- PE is defined relative to a chosen reference point; only changes in PE are physically meaningful.

### 2.3 Conservation of Mechanical Energy
- If only conservative forces (gravity, spring) act (no friction/air resistance/external work):
  **KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂**
- If non-conservative forces act, energy "lost" = work done against friction, etc.

### 2.4 Momentum and Impulse
- **Momentum**: p = mv (vector).
- **Impulse**: J = FΔt = Δp (impulse equals change in momentum).
- **Conservation of momentum**: total momentum of an isolated system is constant (no external net force):
  Σp(before) = Σp(after)
- **Fission** (one object splits into two): momentum conserved, total mass conserved.
- **Coalescence** (two objects combine): momentum conserved, KE generally not conserved (perfectly inelastic).

### 2.5 Collisions
- **Coefficient of restitution**: e = −(v₁' − v₂')/(v₁ − v₂), where primes denote velocities after collision.
  - e = 1: perfectly elastic (KE conserved)
  - 0 < e < 1: inelastic (some KE lost)
  - e = 0: perfectly inelastic (objects stick together)
- **Elastic collision**: both momentum and KE conserved.
- **Inelastic collision**: momentum conserved, KE not conserved (converted to heat, sound, deformation).

---

## 3. Various Forces and Motions

### 3.1 Uniform Circular Motion
- **Angular velocity**: ω = Δθ/Δt = v/r (rad/s).
- **Period and frequency**: T = 2π/ω; f = 1/T.
- **Centripetal acceleration**: a = v²/r = ω²r, directed toward the center.
- **Centripetal force**: F = mv²/r = mω²r (net force causing circular motion — supplied by tension, gravity, friction, normal force, etc., depending on context).
- Non-uniform circular motion has both centripetal (radial) and tangential acceleration components.

### 3.2 Inertial Force
- In a non-inertial (accelerating) reference frame, a **pseudo-force** must be added to make Newton's laws apply.
- **Centrifugal force**: the outward inertial force felt in a rotating frame, magnitude mω²r, opposite direction to centripetal force. It's not a "real" interaction force — it exists only in the rotating observer's frame.

### 3.3 Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)
- Defined by a restoring force proportional to displacement and directed opposite to it: F = −kx.
- **Equations** (starting from x = 0 or x = A depending on setup):
  - x = A sin(ωt) or A cos(ωt)
  - v = Aω cos(ωt), a = −Aω² sin(ωt) = −ω²x
- **Angular frequency**: ω = √(k/m) for a spring-mass system.
- **Period**:
  - Spring pendulum: T = 2π√(m/k)
  - Simple pendulum (small angle): T = 2π√(L/g)
- **Energy in SHM**: Total E = ½kA² = ½mv² + ½kx² (constant); KE = PE when x = A/√2.
- Amplitude (A), period (T), frequency (f), phase, angular frequency (ω) all describe the oscillation.

### 3.4 Universal Gravitation
- **Newton's law of gravitation**: F = GMm/r² (G = gravitational constant).
- **Kepler's Laws**:
  1. Planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus.
  2. A line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times (⇒ planet moves fastest at perihelion, slowest at aphelion).
  3. T² ∝ r³ (for circular orbits: T²/r³ = constant for all bodies orbiting the same central mass).
- **Gravitational potential energy**: U = −GMm/r (zero reference at infinity; negative because gravity is attractive).
- **Orbital speed (circular orbit)**: from GMm/r² = mv²/r ⇒ v = √(GM/r).
- Conservation of mechanical energy applies: KE + U = constant along an orbit.

# PART II — THERMODYNAMICS

## 1. Heat and Temperature

### 1.1 Heat and Temperature
- **Thermal motion**: random kinetic motion of molecules; temperature is a measure of average molecular kinetic energy.
- **Thermal equilibrium**: two bodies in contact reach the same temperature; no net heat flow.
- **Absolute temperature**: T(K) = t(°C) + 273. Absolute zero (0 K = −273°C) is the theoretical minimum.
- **Heat quantity (Q)**: energy transferred due to temperature difference, measured in joules.
- **Heat capacity (C)**: Q = CΔT (energy to raise an object's temperature by 1 K).
- **Specific heat (c)**: heat capacity per unit mass: Q = mcΔT.
- **Conservation of heat**: in an isolated system, heat lost by hotter object = heat gained by cooler object (calorimetry).

### 1.2 States of Matter
- **Three states**: solid, liquid, gas — determined by molecular arrangement/energy.
- **Melting point / boiling point**: temperatures at which solid⇌liquid and liquid⇌gas transitions occur at constant pressure.
- **Latent heat**: energy absorbed/released during a phase change **at constant temperature** (no temperature change while the phase change occurs).
  - Heat of fusion: latent heat for solid⇌liquid.
  - Heat of evaporation (vaporization): latent heat for liquid⇌gas.
- **Thermal expansion**: most materials expand when heated (linear/volume expansion coefficients).

### 1.3 Heat and Work
- **Internal energy (U)**: total kinetic + potential energy of a system's molecules.
- **First law of thermodynamics**: ΔU = Q − W, where Q = heat added to the system, W = work done **by** the system on surroundings.
  *(Watch sign conventions — some texts write ΔU = Q + W with W = work done ON the system. Be consistent within a problem.)*
- **Irreversible change**: a process that cannot spontaneously reverse without external influence (most real processes: friction, free expansion, mixing).
- **Heat engine**: a device converting heat into work in a cyclic process; absorbs Q_h from hot reservoir, expels Q_c to cold reservoir, does work W = Q_h − Q_c.
- **Thermal efficiency**: η = W/Q_h = (Q_h − Q_c)/Q_h = 1 − Q_c/Q_h.
- **Second law of thermodynamics** (qualitative): heat cannot spontaneously flow from cold to hot without external work; no engine can convert heat entirely into work with no other effect (100% efficiency is impossible for a real cyclic engine).

## 2. Properties of Gas

### 2.1 Equation of State of Ideal Gas
- **Boyle's law** (constant T): PV = constant.
- **Charles's law** (constant P): V/T = constant.
- **Boyle–Charles's law** (combined): PV/T = constant.
- **Ideal gas equation of state**: PV = nRT (n = moles, R = gas constant ≈ 8.3 J/(mol·K)).

### 2.2 Motion of Gas Molecules (Kinetic Theory)
- Gas pressure arises from molecules colliding with container walls and transferring momentum.
- Pressure and absolute temperature are both related to the average kinetic energy of molecules: higher T ⇒ higher average molecular speed ⇒ higher pressure (at fixed volume).
- **Internal energy of an ideal gas** depends only on temperature (not volume or pressure):
  - Monatomic gas: U = (3/2)nRT
  - Diatomic gas: U = (5/2)nRT (extra rotational degrees of freedom)

### 2.3 Change of State of Gases
| Process | Held constant | Key relation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isochoric | Volume | W = 0 | Q = ΔU (all heat changes internal energy) |
| Isobaric | Pressure | W = PΔV | Q = ΔU + W |
| Isothermal | Temperature | ΔU = 0 | Q = W (all heat converts to work, ideal gas) |
| Adiabatic | No heat exchange | Q = 0 | ΔU = −W; expansion cools gas, compression heats it |

- **Molar specific heat**: heat required per mole per kelvin; differs for constant-volume (Cv) vs constant-pressure (Cp) processes — Cp > Cv because at constant pressure some energy goes into doing work as the gas expands.

## Quick Formula Reference

**Mechanics**
- v = v₀ + at, x = v₀t + ½at², v² = v₀² + 2ax
- F = ma, W = Fd cosθ, KE = ½mv², P_grav = mgh, P_spring = ½kx²
- p = mv, J = FΔt = Δp
- a_c = v²/r = ω²r, F_c = mv²/r
- T_spring = 2π√(m/k), T_pendulum = 2π√(L/g)
- F_grav = GMm/r², U_grav = −GMm/r, T² ∝ r³

**Thermodynamics**
- T(K) = t(°C) + 273
- Q = mcΔT
- ΔU = Q − W (first law)
- η = W/Q_h = 1 − Q_c/Q_h
- PV = nRT
- U_mono = (3/2)nRT, U_diatomic = (5/2)nRT
